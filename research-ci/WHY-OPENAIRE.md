# What OpenAIRE Cares About (And Why It Aligns)

## Six signals from OpenAIRE's current strategy

### 1. Research intelligence has become the product

OpenAIRE argues research intelligence must be open, trustworthy, inspectable and governed because its assumptions influence real funding/assessment/policy decisions. Pāṭala extends that downstream: analyses derived from intelligence should remain auditable as the intelligence changes.

### 2. Trust is a system property, not a badge

OpenAIRE repeatedly says trustworthy research information must be intentionally designed into infrastructure. Pāṭala's architecture (observation ≠ assertion ≠ qualified state, machine proposal ≠ human authority) matches their institutional philosophy.

### 3. They are already thinking continuously

OpenAIRE published "Why Continuous Validation Matters." Their argument: data gathered once immediately starts becoming stale. Pāṭala extends this: what about conclusions derived from that data?

### 4. Their best case studies are feedback loops

SciLifeLab: deploy → compare → validate → discover mismatch → improve → deploy. OpenOrgs: algorithm proposes → human curates → feedback improves. Pāṭala's (machine proposal → proof obligation → human judgment → append-only event) mirrors this.

### 5. AI agents are a strategic priority

Alien/MCP integration is fresh. OpenAIRE says the next problem after openness is helping agents make sense of volume. What happens to agent memory when authoritative sources change?

### 6. They want reuse, not duplication

Innovation programme: use what works, extend existing assets, bring domain expertise while OpenAIRE brings data/infrastructure/community. Pāṭala = downstream epistemic layer, not competing graph.

## The pilot proposition

A pilot connecting Research CI to Graph releases/Broker events and the Alien research-agent workflow, testing whether persistent AI research outputs can remain synchronized with an evolving scholarly record.

## Why OpenAIRE should care

Pāṭala needs OpenAIRE to be good. It doesn't compete with their infrastructure — it closes the loop between their intelligence and its consumers. That makes it a plausible pilot partnership, not another API wrapper.
